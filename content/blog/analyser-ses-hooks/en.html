The hook is the only part of your video that everyone sees. A Buffer study of 1.1 million TikTok videos measured a median watch time of 3.1 seconds for 5-to-10-second videos, and 6.9 seconds for 30-to-60-second videos (Buffer, 2025). For a large part of your audience, the hook is the video. Here is how to measure it, break it down and improve it.

What is a hook on TikTok Shop?

It is the opening of the video, the moment the viewer decides to stay or scroll. TikTok Shop Academy, in its guide "Content Styles to Help Your Creative Process on TikTok Shop" published in May 2026, splits a selling video into three parts: the hook (the first 3 to 6 seconds), the key message, then the call to action. The guide specifies that the hook should relate to the featured product, so the transition into the key message feels natural (PPC Land).

That last point is what separates a TikTok Shop hook from an entertainment hook. An opening that keeps people watching but has nothing to do with the product inflates your views and sinks your click rate: people stay for one thing and get sold another.

How do you measure a hook's performance?

The reference metric is the hook rate: the number of viewers still watching at 3 seconds divided by the number of views. In TikTok Studio, you read it on each video's retention curve.

For context: Motion, a creative analytics platform for ads, says a hook rate below 25% "needs work", 25 to 35% is solid, and above 35% captures interest quickly (Motion). These benchmarks come from video advertising; for organic content, your own median across your last twenty videos is still the best reference.

Judge a hook on two numbers, not one. The hook rate tells you whether it stops the scroll; the product click rate tells you whether it attracted the right people. A hook with a 50% hook rate and a 0.5% click rate is often less profitable than one at 30% and 3%.

How do you break a hook down, layer by layer?

A hook is not a sentence. It is four layers stacked during the first seconds. To analyse one, watch it four times, isolating one layer each time:

LayerQuestion to askWeak signal
ImageWhat is on the very first frame? The product, a face, a result?An empty shot, a logo, someone getting settled
On-screen textCan the promise be read without sound in under two seconds?No text, or a sentence that is too long
SpeechDoes the first sentence name a problem, a result or a contradiction?"Hey everyone", "So today…"
Movement / soundIs there an action or a sound that creates a break?Static shot and neutral background music

Hooks that perform line up at least three layers behind the same promise. Weak hooks often have only one layer doing the work — typically a good sentence spoken over a dull shot, which nobody hears because the video plays muted.

Which types of hooks work for selling?

TikTok's own guidance recommends opening with a question, a relatable statement or a bold claim, and showing the product clearly through an unboxing, close-ups or a demonstration (TikTok for Business). In practice, there are six families:

  1. The named problem: "If your hair frizzes the moment it rains…" — the viewer who has that problem feels addressed.
  2. Result first: show the after before explaining. The before/after format, common in beauty, cleaning and fitness.
  3. The contradiction: "Don't buy this product if…" — the objection becomes the hook.
  4. The instant demo: the product is in action from the first frame, without a word.
  5. The direct question: "Did you know you've been emptying your vacuum wrong for years?" — as long as the answer is the product.
  6. Social proof: "The product you all asked me about" — works best when your audience already knows you.

No family wins everywhere. The right type depends on the category, the price and how aware your audience is of the problem — which is why you should look at what works in your niche before choosing.

Example: two hooks for the same product

Example (made-up numbers, to illustrate the reading) — A creator posts two almost identical videos about a straightening brush; only the hook changes.

Hook A: the creator on camera saying "I tested something incredible". Hook rate 22%, product click rate 1%.

Hook B: first frame on a frizzy strand, text "3 minutes, no flat iron", the brush enters the frame at second two. Hook rate 41%, product click rate 3.2%.

Reading: A states neither the problem nor the product; no layer is working. B lines up image (the problem), text (the quantified result) and movement (the product arriving) behind the same promise. The click gain comes from B filtering for the right people from the start.

How do you fix a hook that doesn't work?

  • Cut the first second. In many videos it is used to get settled. Trimming the start is sometimes enough to raise the hook rate.
  • Put the product in the opening frame. TikTok Shop Academy's guide says it: the hook should relate to the product.
  • Write the promise on screen. Short, readable in two seconds, consistent with what is said.
  • Test one thing. Repost the same video with a single different hook: it is the only way to know what caused the difference.
  • Check what follows. If the hook rate rises but retention collapses right after, your hook promises something the video doesn't deliver.
